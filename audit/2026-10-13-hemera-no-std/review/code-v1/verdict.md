## Closest call: the benchmark gate

I considered blocking on this. §20's self-verification clause requires "benchmark — no regressions vs baselines," and §28/§29 show measured slower cases. I am not blocking, for these reasons, and I want the reasoning on record because it is the one place a future reader could mistake this APPROVE for a performance clearance.

The data does not describe a code change; it describes an uncontrolled host. §47 (parent repeat) reports `pattern/add -19%`, `mul -18%`, `hash -27%`, `hash_chain_64 -47%`, `encode/tree_64 -54%`, `encode/particle_atom -53%` *against the parent's own earlier run of identical source*. The parent moved by up to 54% with zero bytes changed. That is the noise floor of this host, and it is larger than every candidate-vs-parent delta in §29 except `encode_tree_64` (+89.6%). §28 and §29 also disagree in *direction* on `add` (+23.7% then −36.4%), `mul` (+12.0% then −10.1%), `jet_merkle_verify` (+16.9% then −2.0%), `encode_particle_atom` (−4.8% then +43.1%) — sign-flipping is the signature of environmental variance, not of a feature flag. §29's own 95% intervals are internally unstable: parent `pattern/inv` spans 64.9–75.2 µs in §29 but §47's raw Criterion prints `[72.314 µs 106.20 µs 162.79 µs]` for the same run, a >2× spread.

Crucially, the execution note does **not** make the argument I just made. §3 says "cause is unestablished," "not explained away as noise," "the shared host was not a controlled benchmark lab," "no further repeat or source tuning is hidden," and §30 says equal 3028144-byte sizes with unequal whole-file hashes "does not localize a performance cause." §29's disposition field says "remains red/unverified." That restraint is exactly what the plan's step 5 demanded and what makes the record trustworthy. Approving a *disclosed red* is a different act from approving a waived one, and the owner — not I — holds the disposition. I am approving the source repair with the gate red and stated; I am not certifying performance.
